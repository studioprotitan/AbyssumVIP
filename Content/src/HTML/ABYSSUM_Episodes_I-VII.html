Episode I
Surface
"Ash Before Dawn"
Corgemont Station — Freight Bay 17 — Pre-Departure
Cold Open
The rails scream.
Not steel under freight weight.
The sound that means something alive is moving underneath.
INT. CORGEMONT STATION — FREIGHT BAY 17 — NIGHT
Surface — Fog-Level / Maintenance Lamps Active
Close on: a weld. BRAE GRINDSTONE's hands, glove cracked at the knuckle from ten years of this. The arc light pulses. Everything smells like soot and coolant.
She pauses. Listens.
The rails stop screaming.
The repair bay lights tick in yellow pulses above her. Steam leaks from a cracked coolant pipe two feet over her head. She has not fixed it. She stopped caring about pipes at Corgemont six months ago when she made the deal.
Behind her: Freight Car Bay 17. The crate. Black alloy, forge-sealed, still faintly warm despite everything. Its heat has nothing to do with transit. It has been warm since before she accepted the job.
Brae puts down the welder. Turns.
She pulls the card from her jacket. Thin slab of polished alloy. The AFFILIATES' insignia pressed into it with the kind of precision that means someone cares about the impression it makes.
Her Mint-to-Deploy Authorization.
A whole train's worth of lives in something lighter than a transit token.
The crate responds to the card's proximity. Mechanical sigh. Locks disengaging. Panels folding open.
Inside: the GLITCH GOBLIN.
Compact. Still. Built from the ash residue of Kaiju bone burned at extreme temperature, fused with ore from the Cenote caverns below the southern flood plain. The resulting alloy is harder than the floor it rests on and carries a permanent residual heat that has nothing to do with temperature.
It was never alive. There is nothing in it to feel bad about.
Brae has been telling herself this for three weeks.
BRAE
quiet, to herself
Don't worry. I've got the leash.
She raises the Mint Card toward the deployment port.
Floor 784. High enough to survive the Rift Flood. High enough that the fog predators can't reach it. The apartment is real. She has seen the listing. She has been thinking about it for three months.
Sirens aboard the train — hardened, fire-capable — will be run through the Affiliates' unauthorized furnace chambers. What comes out wrong will be called Glitch Witches by the people who survive long enough to name them. The Forge Mystics will be processed into Deadman Alchemists.
The train has thirty cars.
Brae's hand doesn't move.
She notices this about herself a moment later.
INT. FREIGHT BAY 17 — CONTINUOUS
From deep in the station — below the station — a sound begins. Not machinery. Not rail predators.
A harmonic vibration that moves through metal rather than air. Through the weld scars on Brae's hands. Through the station bones.
The Goblin's furnace core pulses.
Once.
The Mint Card wasn't even close to the activation port.
BRAE
That's not right.
Military Glitches don't wake without the card. That is the whole architecture of the control system. The Affiliates paid for that certainty. She was bought, in part, because of that certainty.
The Goblin's eyes ignite. Twin embers.
Forge script cycles across its chassis — not a startup sequence. A response. It is responding to something Brae cannot hear.
Every terminal in the repair dock flickers to life simultaneously.
All of them. The same message.
FORGE NETWORK HANDSHAKE DETECTED
THREAT SIGNATURE: ACTIVE
RESPONSE: INITIATED
The station intercom crackles.
MARROW VEX
through static — calm, measured, corporate
Miss Grindstone. Your hesitation has been noted. Deploy the unit. The train will not wait.
Outside: the departure horn. One long mournful blast. The kind engineers use when they already know the route ahead is cursed.
Brae looks at the Goblin.
The Goblin looks at the Mint Card.
Its optics narrow — not recognition. Calibration. It is assessing the card as a variable in a logic system that is running on a source neither of them has access to.
Brae looks at the thirty-car train through the freight bay doors. The CST escorts walking the platform with shock rifles they believe are precautionary.
She looks at the Mint Card.
She snaps it in half.
The Goblin stands motionless. Three seconds of logic processing. Card destroyed. Deployment: aborted. Affiliate command chain: severed. Forge Network handshake: still active.
The network fills the gap.
MARROW VEX
through static — quieter now, which is worse
That was a very expensive mistake, Miss Grindstone.
Somewhere above the station: the train begins to pull away.
Somewhere below the station: the harmonic deepens.
On the floor of the repair bay — a seam Brae has never noticed before. Faintly glowing. Running from beneath her feet toward the wall.
She crouches.
Ancient runes along the edge. Forge script older than anything running on the current network.
With a grinding sound that has been waiting a long time to happen, the platform opens.
Cold air rushes up. Not sewer air. Not flood rot. Dry and hot at the same time — the breath of a furnace that has been sealed for centuries.
Brae looks down into the darkness.
The Goblin steps forward beside her and looks down too.
Both of them. Looking at the same darkness. For entirely different reasons.
End of Episode I
The train is gone. The Affiliates know what she did. Floor 784 is gone with it. What's left is a hole in the floor and something below it that woke up without being asked.

Episode II
Surface Level 1
"Below the Bone"
Corgemont — Sub-Station Levels — Pipefitter Corridors
Cold Open
The Goblin's foot enters the standing water.
It has never touched water before.
It stops. Assesses. Continues.
Something in the water moves away from it.
INT. CORGEMONT — SPIRAL STAIR — NIGHT
Surface-Level / Descending
The stair carved into rail-blackened stone. No end to it visible from above. Brae descends with one hand on a handrail etched with symbols she's never seen before — pressure valves designed to look like seed pods. Root bifurcations cast in the shape of pipe junctions.
The Goblin descends behind her. No handrail needed. No fear of depth. It processes and moves.
The heat increases with each step.
Then: her boot hits water.
She freezes. Looks down.
The stair continues into standing black water. Ankle deep. The water is still. The water is warm.
Flood Level 1 — Ankle / 0.3m standing water
FLOOD LEVEL 1. The Rift Flood has reached the maintenance corridors below Corgemont. The engineers who built these stairs didn't plan for this. Nobody did.
Something moves through the water ahead of her. Small. Fast. Gone before she can track it.
Encounter — Flood Level 1 / Corgemont Sub-Station
Bilge Scuttlers
Small corrupted Forge automatons — originally maintenance units designed to operate in crawlways. The flood water reached their housing three years ago. They adapted. Their chassis now runs on thermal differential rather than Forge Network signal — the heat from the pipes above warms the water, which powers them. They are not aggressive. They are territorial. They have never seen anything walk into their water voluntarily. They will decide whether this is a threat or an anomaly. That decision will take approximately forty seconds. Brae does not have forty seconds.
Brae keeps moving. Through the water. Not slow — walking through shin-deep resistance at pace. The Goblin behind her creates a wake.
The Bilge Scuttlers decide: threat.
They come from the walls.
Brae uses what she has: her welder, her repair kit used as a bludgeon, the Goblin interceding with the efficiency of something built for exactly this purpose. Not a fight. A transit problem. She's not trying to win — she's trying to reach the end of the corridor.
She reaches it.
INT. CORGEMONT — PIPEFITTER ACCESS CORRIDOR — CONTINUOUS
Flood Level 1 — Shin / Drying toward Forge heat
The corridor runs beside a massive pipe — hot enough to feel from three feet. The water level drops as the floor rises toward the Forge heat source. Brae's boots leave wet prints that dry within steps.
The walls here are different. Pressure gauges and valve wheels, yes — but also inscriptions. Too old to be maintenance markings. Too deliberate to be accidental.
She passes a row of sealed iron doors.
ARCHIVE TOMB — SERIES 04.
WEAPONS CRYPT.
The doors extend as far as she can see. Too many to count. Too many for a substation. Too many for anything she has a word for.
BRAE
under her breath
This isn't a power plant.
The Goblin raises its hand. The map projection activates — the rail lines and station nodes she saw in the repair bay — and then expands. And keeps expanding. Node points multiplying past the edge of the render, extending beneath the entire southern route like the root system of a forest that extends past all horizons.
Not a hidden station.
A parallel civilization of infrastructure.
Older than the surface lines. Older than the stations above them. Possibly older than any of the names for what it is.
Brae stares at it for a long time.
BRAE
How large is this?
The projection doesn't answer. It just shows more.
At the far end of the corridor: a door different from the others. Larger. Circular. The kind of seal designed not to keep things out but to contain what's inside until it's needed.
Stenciled above it:
HYPERCHAMBER ACCESS
The water on the floor has completely dried. The air here is hot and still. The Forge hum is loudest here — less a sound than a pressure in the inner ear.
Between Brae and the Hyperchamber door: another corridor junction. Water visible down the left branch. Not ankle deep.
Flood Level 2 — Chest Deep / Left branch to Hyperchamber approach
The direct route to the Hyperchamber runs through it.
Brae looks at the water. At the faint bioluminescent glow beginning on the walls where the flood reaches. Something living has colonized the lower level. Something that makes its own light.
Beautiful. Wrong.
End of Episode II
The Hyperchamber door is forty meters ahead. Thirty of those meters are chest-deep in something that glows. She can't go back — Marrow Vex's people will have reached the surface entry point by now. The only direction is through.

Episode III
Level 2 Below the Flood
"The Stone That Remembers"
Corgemont — Flood Level 2 Approach — Hyperchamber
Cold Open
The node stone is already glowing when she finds it.
It has been here long enough to synchronize with the engines.
She doesn't know how long that is.
Neither does anyone else.
INT. CORGEMONT — FLOOD LEVEL 2 APPROACH CORRIDOR
Flood Level 2 — 1.4m standing / bioluminescent colonization
She goes in.
The water hits her chest immediately. Cold below, warm near the surface where the pipe heat carries down. The bioluminescent growth on the walls provides the only light — blue-green filaments in fractal patterns that map exactly to the Forge script above the waterline, as if the fungus read the inscription and decided to continue it.
Brae moves through it. Arms raised, welder held above the surface.
Something large moves at the far edge of visibility. Below the surface. Deeper than the corridor should be.
Encounter — Flood Level 2 / Sub-Archive Corridor
Archive Warden
A Forge maintenance unit that sealed itself inside the Archive Tomb corridor when the flood reached this level. It has been running on thermal energy and residual Forge signal for three years in complete darkness. It adapted. Its chassis grew into the walls — it now partially IS the corridor, with limbs that can extend from wall panels up to six meters. It does not attack immediately. It observes. It has been alone for three years and does not know what she is. The solution is not combat — it is demonstration. Brae carries Forge-adjacent tools. Her welder leaves a heat signature the Warden recognizes as authorized. She passes. She is not certain how.
On the other side of the flooded corridor: a chamber. Dry. The water stops at the threshold as if something holds it back.
The Goblin emerges beside her. Its chassis logs have recorded the passage. The Forge script cycling across its frame has shifted frequency — it has learned something from this transit that it did not know before.
INT. CORGEMONT — PIPEFITTER NODE ALCOVE — CONTINUOUS
Below the Flood — Dry / Forge Heat Active
Set into the floor of the alcove: a row of small depressions. Each containing a stone. Still hot — visibly radiating.
Most are dull. One is not.
The glowing one pulses in a pattern almost too slow to track. Brae counts it: the same rhythm as the engines above. It has been here long enough to synchronize completely.
She crouches beside it. Feels the heat from three inches away — the warmth of something that has been actively maintaining its temperature for an unknown number of years.
The old Forge Mystics called these node stones. Said Sirens could retrieve them. Said the stones eventually became what a Siren was at the center. Said rare ones made a Siren into something the scale of which didn't translate well into language.
She had thought that was mystical nonsense.
She picks it up.
The heat travels through her palm and keeps going.
INT. CORGEMONT — HYPERCHAMBER — CONTINUOUS
The chamber door opens when she approaches. Doesn't unlock — simply opens. As if it has been waiting for this specific variable to arrive.
Inside: a chamber shaped like the inside of a heart. Not metaphorically. The architecture was designed by something that understood cardiac function and applied it to stone. Massive valve-wheels. Pressure chambers on either side of a central passage. Everything designed to move something through itself at regular intervals.
The valve-wheels begin to turn when Brae enters. The Forge engines above shift rhythm — less background, more purposeful. The hum drops in frequency until it travels through bone rather than air.
The node stone in her hand stops feeling like stone.
It starts feeling like her.
The process is not comfortable. The Furnace exists to harden. It finds every weak point and either burns it away or rebuilds it into something load-bearing. Brae's mechanic's harness reshapes itself — threads of the stone's material moving through the fabric, fusing layers without destroying them. Her welding gloves become gauntlets. The tools at her belt lock into newly formed sockets.
Nothing is wasted. Everything adapts.
The Forge is practical that way.
When she opens her eyes, the world has more information in it.
She can see the Ley lines. Golden energy moving through the walls like rivers — the network's own circulation. They do not respond to her gaze. They are not interested in her. They have been running since before she existed and will continue after.
Her gauntlets respond when she flexes her hands. Glyphs appear above her palm — a control interface she wasn't taught. She has the tools. Mastery was not included.
Then: the Hyperchamber network.
She feels all of them simultaneously. Like knowing where every subway station exists without a map. Dozens. Hundreds. The sub-network of transit nodes running beneath the entire southern line, predating the surface rail by orders of magnitude.
She turns slowly, reading the network.
Half the nodes are dark. Flooded. The Rift Flood reached the transit shafts between them. The shafts that lead toward Stones Throw are —
She stops.
BRAE
All three of them are fully submerged.
She tunes to the nearest transit path — the frequency of the Hyperchamber it connects to, forty kilometers toward Stones Throw.
Something in the transit shaft moves when it registers her signal.
It moves toward her frequency.
Then stops.
Waiting.
End of Episode III
She is a Siren. She can traverse the Hyperchamber network. The path to Stones Throw is fully submerged. Something lives in it. Something that just felt her arrive and decided to wait rather than approach. She does not know if that means she is safe. She knows only one thing: the network is waiting for her to move.

Episode IV
Level 3 — Submerged
"What Lives in Level Three"
Hyperchamber Transit Shaft — Fully Submerged / Between Corgemont and Stones Throw
Cold Open
She goes in anyway.
INT. HYPERCHAMBER TRANSIT SHAFT — FLOOD LEVEL 3
Flood Level 3 — Fully Submerged / 8m+ standing / inhabited
The transit begins as movement through dark water toward a light she can already see — the destination Hyperchamber's resonance signature, visible to her Core as a warm point in the dark. The network does the rest.
Then: the shaft proper. She is inside it.
Complete submersion. The flood water has been in this shaft for years — long enough for the ecosystem to establish. The bioluminescence here is not the tentative wall-growth of Flood Level 2. It is a complete replacement of all Forge-manufactured light. Everything that was artificial is now biological.
Node stones on the shaft floor are still pulsing. Still warm. The water around them moves with the thermal current they generate. Small things orbit them — adapted organisms, drawn to the heat, too small and too many to count.
Brae can breathe. Siren physiology — the node stone's integration — has handled this. She does not know how. She does not have time to think about how.
Because ahead: the shape.
Encounter — Flood Level 3 / Transit Shaft 7-C
The Depth Current — Class Unknown
There is no designation in the surface catalogues for what inhabits the Flood Level 3 transit shafts. It has been there since the water arrived. It may have arrived with the water.

Scale: fills the shaft. Not by accident — by choice. It is aware of the shaft's dimensions and occupies them deliberately.

It is not hostile. It is evaluating. Siren Core frequency is a type of signal it has encountered before — in what form, from what previous Siren, and how long ago are questions the story does not yet answer.

It makes a decision about Brae. The decision is: not today.

It moves aside.

This is more frightening than attack would have been. Attack she could process. This requires her to understand that something ancient and vast reviewed her existence and chose to let it continue. She does not know its criteria. She does not know if she met them or if she was simply beneath notice.
The thing holds position.
She moves past it.
She does not run. Running in water is inefficient and she is not going to let it see inefficiency. She moves at pace. She does not look back. She does not look at it. She moves toward the destination frequency and does not stop until —
INT. TRANSIT MIDPOINT HYPERCHAMBER — PARTIAL FLOOD
Flood Level 1 — Ankle / Midpoint Node between Corgemont and Stones Throw
She surfaces. Her boots find stone. Ankle-deep water. The bioluminescence from the Level 3 transit still visible through the floor grating below — a deep blue glow, steady.
The Goblin emerges beside her, running through its damage assessment. Chassis intact. Logic systems intact. It experienced the transit differently — as a network handshake rather than a physical journey. It processed the transit. It did not process the thing in the water.
Brae stands still for a moment.
Her breathing is steady because she has decided it will be.
BRAE
to herself, very quiet
Okay. That was a thing that exists.
She checks her gauntlet interface. The network shows the midpoint node — she is halfway between Corgemont and Stones Throw. The next transit shaft is also Level 3. The thing she passed has not moved from the first shaft. It is still there.
She can feel it registered in the network as an anomaly — a presence the Forge Network catalogues but does not classify. A variable it encounters and records without resolving.
Above her — through the stone ceiling of the midpoint chamber — the distant sound of surface rail. She is beneath the southern line. Somewhere above: the infrastructure of the world she came from.
She looks at the second transit shaft.
She looks at her hands. The gauntlets. The glyph interface still faintly visible.
She looks at the shaft.
BRAE
Second time's easier.
It is not easier.
But she comes through it.
INT. STONES THROW APPROACH — SUB-PLATFORM EMERGENCE SHAFT
Surface-Adjacent / Below Stones Throw Station
She emerges into a dry chamber. Above her: a maintenance shaft leading upward. Through a grating in the ceiling, weak light. The orange-wrong light of Stones Throw maintenance lamps.
She is below the station.
She climbs.
She surfaces onto a rail platform in the station's lower yard — and the first thing she sees is the Goblin swarm.
Hundreds of optics, all igniting at once. Red, then settling to amber. Row after row of constructed things turning to face her. The rail yard between the towers, full of them. All registering the same Forge Network signal she carries.
All going still.
Brae stands on the platform.
The Goblin from Corgemont emerges behind her and takes position at her left shoulder.
The swarm holds.
End of Episode IV
She survived Level 3 twice. She is at Stones Throw. The Goblin swarm is waiting for her to move. In the gantry above: three silhouettes looking down. The station doesn't know what to do with her. Neither does she.

Surface — Station Level / Fog Active / Three Towers

EXT. STONES THROW — RAIL YARD — CONTINUOUS
Encounter — Surface / Approach Viaduct — Prior to Arrival
Railbone Crawler — Adult
The viaduct connecting Stones Throw's middle tower to the southern approach is inhabited. The Railbone Crawler — a semi-mechanical territorial predator formed from fused rail debris and platform wreckage — uses rail infrastructure as both highway and hunting ground. It detected Brae's approach through the sub-surface transit vibrations. It does not know what she is. It knows the vibration pattern means something moved through its territory. The viaduct approach is the gauntlet she had to clear to reach the rail yard. She used the Goblin swarm as tactical cover. The Crawler pursued. It stopped when the swarm turned to face it. It does not understand what has changed at this station. It retreats to the viaduct. It will return.
The rail yard.
Brae walks through the kneeling machines.
Row after row. Hundreds of constructed things, each built from the residue of burning Kaiju bone, each running on the same network signal she carries. None of them hers. All of them responding to the network priority she represents.
She does not slow down. She walks through them at pace. The Goblin from Corgemont at her left shoulder, the only unit in the yard that has been with her since before this.
In the gantry above: three faces at the glass.
Brae stops in the center of the yard. The Goblin swarm holds position. Steam from the station towers drifts through the floodlights.
She lifts her head.
She looks directly at the gantry window.
From this distance she cannot see their faces clearly. But she can see the way they have stopped moving. The particular stillness of people who have realized a situation is not what they thought.
BRAE
Marrow Vex.
Her voice carries. The Goblin swarm amplifies it — hundreds of units emitting the same resonant tone, sending the word through the station metal, through the towers, through the infrastructure of a place that has been dying for years.
The swarm begins walking. Forward. Toward the station.

at the window

She's not controlling them. They're following her.

RAEL
Then we have a problem.
On the terminal behind them — the name still glowing in the Forge Network identification log:
BRAE GRINDSTONE
SIREN CLASS — ACTIVE  ·  ORIGIN NODE: CORGEMONT
End of Episode V
She is in the yard. The swarm is moving. Three CST operatives are deciding whether to engage something the network says is on their side. Marrow Vex is at Styx Station and now knows exactly where she is. The name is on the terminal. She is no longer anonymous.

Episode VI
Surface Level 2
"The Song of Iron"
Stones Throw Station — Gantry / Lower Furnace Levels
Cold Open
Lyra's face at the gantry glass.
She recognizes Brae Grindstone.
Then she recognizes what Brae Grindstone is now.
Those are two different things.
INT. STONES THROW — GANTRY CONTROL — NIGHT
The three of them come down from the gantry. Not because Brae forced them to — because Rael made the tactical assessment: the Goblin swarm is between them and every exit. Descending to negotiate is more survivable than fortifying a glass room.
They meet in the lower yard. Brae. The swarm at distance. The Goblin from Corgemont at her shoulder.
Lyra looks at her for a long moment.
LYRA
You were on the transfer manifest. Corgemont repair crew. You were supposed to be on that train.
BRAE
I was supposed to do a lot of things tonight.
RAEL
not hostile — reading the situation
The Forge Network activated a Siren class at Corgemont. You're telling me that was you.
BRAE
I'm telling you the network made a decision and I was standing in the right place when it did. Or the wrong place. I haven't decided which.
TORCH
The swarm. You're running them?
BRAE
The network is running them. I'm the node they're orienting to. That's not the same thing.
A beat. Rael looks at the swarm. At Brae. At the Goblin that is clearly not behaving like an Affiliate-deployed unit.
RAEL
What do you need from this station?
BRAE
To understand what the Affiliates have been running down here. And then to get to Styx.
Lyra and Rael exchange a look. The kind that carries a conversation.
LYRA
Then you need to see the lower levels.
INT. STONES THROW — LOWER FURNACE APPROACH — NIGHT
Flood Level 2 — Affiliate-operated sub-levels / unauthorized Furnace access
The Affiliates have been using the station's sub-platform access for months. The evidence is everywhere — unauthorized Forge connection lines running into the maintenance corridors, makeshift processing chambers grafted onto original infrastructure, the specific smell of a Furnace cycle run without proper calibration.
Brae's Ley line perception shows the damage. The network here is wrong — not corrupted by flood water but corrupted by misuse. The Forge lines have been diverted. Their purpose changed. Things have been processed here that should not have been processed here.
She finds the first one in a flooded maintenance shaft.
Chest-deep water. Bioluminescent. The glow wrong — not the clean blue-green of natural colonization. Teal-flame quality. The specific color of a corrupted Siren node stone.
Encounter — Flood Level 2 / Stones Throw Furnace Sub-Level
Glitch Witch — Stones Throw Station
She was a Siren. The Affiliates brought her through the Furnace with a Core damaged past the repair threshold. She did not come out correctly. She did not die. She is still running on fragments of what she was.

She remembers the network. She can almost hear it. What she hears instead is a broken signal — instructions that make sense inside a context that no longer exists. She is acting on those instructions with the full capability of a Siren unit that never lost its structural integrity.

This is what makes Glitch Witches more dangerous than Goblins: Goblins are built for a purpose. A Witch has capability without coherence. She improvises. And what a Siren-class unit improvises in a flooded basement under a condemned station is not something the encounter tables have protocols for.

The horror of the encounter is not survival — Brae survives. The horror is recognition. Through her Siren perception she can see what this was. She can see the Core, still present, broken at the anchor point. She can see what the Furnace was trying to reconstruct and what it rebuilt instead.
After.
Brae stands in the water. Chest-deep. The teal light from the Witch's disrupted Core dissipating.
Lyra is behind her. Torch and Rael at the corridor entrance. All three have seen this before — they work at this station, they have found the others — but watching Brae see it for the first time is different.
Brae's expression has not changed. Her breathing has not changed. But her hands — the gauntlets — are still slightly lit. She has been reading what remains of the Witch's Core for the last thirty seconds.
BRAE
quiet
How many are down here.
Not a question.
LYRA
Seven confirmed. We think there are more. They've been using the flood corridors to move between tower sub-levels.
BRAE
The Affiliates ran them through the Furnace with damaged Cores. Either they didn't know what would happen or they didn't care. I don't know which is worse.
RAEL
What does the Forge Network say?
BRAE
The Forge Network registered this as a threat signature and generated a response. I'm the response. That's all the network's commentary on the matter.
She turns. Moves back toward the surface access.
BRAE
Vex ordered this from Styx Station. Which means Malvek designed it from further out. Clearing this sub-level doesn't close the operation — it removes one field site. The network problem is—
She stops.
Through her Siren perception: the Ley lines running toward the Swamp Wards. Still severed. Still dark. The flood levels in the southern districts have not changed while she has been underground.
BRAE
finishing the sentence
—systemic. I know.
The intercom in the corridor — an Affiliate communications unit the station recovered from the lower levels — crackles to life.
MARROW VEX
through the unit — no static. Clear signal. He is listening.
Miss Grindstone. I was wondering when you'd find the processing levels. I hope the tour was informative.
A pause.
MARROW VEX
Come to Styx Station. You'll want to see what we've been working toward. And you will come — the network you're carrying will insist on it.
The signal cuts.
He is not wrong about the network. She can feel the Styx Hyperchamber in her perception — the Gateway frequency registering through the transit network like a different quality of light. Something is active there that is not Forge-origin. It is drawing the network's attention the way a fault draws stress lines.
She looks at Rael.
BRAE
The Hyperchamber transit to Styx runs through Level 3.
RAEL
We know. We've lost two survey drones in there. Retrieval signal went wrong on both — they arrived at the wrong destination. One of them surfaced three kilometers off course, the other never surfaced.
BRAE
Corrupted network section between here and Styx.
RAEL
Correct.
Brae looks at her gauntlet. The glyph interface. The incomplete mastery she has of something she was given without a manual.
BRAE
A Siren's Core can navigate a corrupted traverse.
She sounds like she is convincing herself.
TORCH
Can it?
BRAE
I'm about to find out.
End of Episode VI
The corrupted Level 3 traverse to Styx Station. The network corruption means arrival at the wrong destination — or no arrival at all. The Depth Current is in there. Something else might be too. Vex is waiting. The Gateway is active. She goes in.

Episode VII
Level 3 — Corrupted Surface Gateway
"What the Gateway Drinks"
Corrupted Transit Shaft → Styx Station → The Gateway
Cold Open
The transit shaft to Styx is darker than the others.
Not the darkness of depth.
The darkness of something wrong happening for a long time
in a place where nothing was supposed to go wrong.
INT. CORRUPTED HYPERCHAMBER TRANSIT SHAFT — FLOOD LEVEL 3
Flood Level 3 — Corrupted Network Section / Transit Shaft 7-D to Styx
The corruption manifests as distortion. The transit that should move her smoothly toward the Styx Hyperchamber resonance signature keeps pulling sideways — toward the wrong frequency, toward a destination that doesn't exist in any catalogue she has access to. She corrects against it. Her Core tuned to the Styx signature. The network fights her on every correction.
The Affiliate interference is deliberate. Someone — Vex, or Malvek's network engineers — has been running corrupted signal through this transit shaft for long enough that the interference has become structural. It is not a jamming system. It is damage done to the network itself.
Brae navigates through it the way she navigated the Level 2 flooding — not by overpowering but by reading. She learned to read pipes and pressure differentials and signal loads in ten years of maintenance work. This is the same logic at a different scale.
Then: the Depth Current. Same entity from the Corgemont-side shaft, or a different one — she cannot tell. It registers her Core frequency. The same evaluation. The same long pause.
The same decision: not today.
But this time something else is in the shaft. Something younger than the Depth Current and less certain. Something that the Affiliates put here deliberately — a modified Goblin unit, chassis adapted for deep water operation, running on corrupted Forge signal. It does not have the Depth Current's wisdom about what a Siren Core means. It attacks.
Encounter — Flood Level 3 / Corrupted Transit Shaft / Affiliate Modification
Deep Warden — Affiliate Deep-Water Goblin Variant
A Glitch Goblin chassis modified for full submersion operation. Running on corrupted Forge signal rather than Mint authorization — the Affiliates found a way to use the network's own infrastructure against itself. Faster than a standard Goblin. Disoriented by the transit corruption — it registered Brae's Siren signal as threat because the interference has been teaching it that Siren signals are hostile. It is wrong. It does not know it is wrong. The fight takes place in complete submersion in the darkest section of the transit shaft. The Depth Current observes without intervening. That fact alone is clarifying.
She arrives.
INT. STYX STATION — HYPERCHAMBER EMERGENCE POINT — NIGHT
Surface — Styx Station Interior / Gateway Active
She surfaces in a dry chamber beneath the station's central hall. Immediately: the Gateway frequency, close enough now to be a physical pressure. Not Forge-origin. Something from the other side.
She climbs toward it.
INT. STYX STATION — CENTRAL HALL — CONTINUOUS
MARROW VEX is waiting.
Not surprised. Not unprepared. He has had time to position, time to bring his remaining field assets, time to set the junction hall as a tactical environment. He is a competent field commander. He uses the time he has the way competent people use it.
He looks at Brae for a moment before anything else.
MARROW VEX
A Corgemont maintenance worker. The network must be running low on options.
BRAE
It's running a lot of options simultaneously. I'm one of them.
MARROW VEX
It won't be enough. You understand that. Whatever the Forge Network built you to do tonight — it cannot change what we've already set in motion at the Gateway. The process is running. Stopping me stops nothing.
BRAE
You keep telling me to stop. I haven't started yet.
The Goblin swarm arrives through the station access corridors — the units from Stones Throw, moving through surface routes while Brae used the transit shaft. Vex's positioning accounts for the swarm. He is good at his work.
It is not enough.
The fight is tactical, brutal, and won by a combination of Siren capability and the specific improvisation of someone who grew up solving problems with whatever was at hand. Vex is a professional. Brae is also a professional — just a different kind, in a different system, with ten years of reading machines that are trying to kill her before anyone called it that.
Vex falls.
The junction hall goes quiet.
The Goblin swarm holds position.
Brae stands in the center of the hall.
She is breathing. That's the main thing.
— The Scene That Must Exist —
She turns and looks at the Gateway.
The central hall of Styx Station was built around it. The entire architectural logic of the space — the orientation of the columns, the height of the ceiling, the position of every load-bearing element — exists to frame this point. Whoever built the station understood what they were building around. They understood it and kept building.
The Gateway is massive. Built from materials that predate the station around it. The arch is Forge-script-covered stone that has been here since before any current map was drawn. The script is not maintenance marking. It is instruction.
Beyond it: movement.
Not through it. Adjacent to it. The way weather moves behind glass. The way pressure changes before a storm arrives. The Enigmatic Universe pressing against this side of the door. Regarding.
Brae stands in front of it and opens her Siren perception fully.
The Ley lines in the hall. Running through the Gateway arch as if it is part of the network — which it is. Running out through the sub-network beneath the station, connecting to the transit shafts she came through, connecting to Stones Throw, connecting to Corgemont, connecting to the hundreds of unmapped nodes that extend past the render.
She follows the lines south.
The Swamp Wards.
The severed nodes are still dark. The Ley lines that should be feeding them power, connecting them to the network, giving the communities below the flood tier access to what the network provides — those lines end in nothing. Severed. Not by tonight's Affiliate operation. By years of systemic failure the Affiliates have been exploiting.
The flood level in the southern districts has not changed.
The people below the evacuation tier are still below it.
She won the fight.
Vex is down. The unauthorized Furnace use at Stones Throw can be stopped. The Goblin deployment authorization has been severed. The Affiliate field operation in the southern network has lost its commander.
The network reads the same.
The Swamp Wards read the same.
She stands in front of the Gateway for a long time.
The movement behind the glass continues. Patient. Regarding. Whatever is on the other side has been there for a very long time and will continue to be there regardless of what Marrow Vex did or didn't accomplish. The Gateway doesn't care about Vex. It was here before the Affiliates named themselves and it will be here after their operation has been dismantled down to the last corrupted network node.
Brae's gauntlet interface registers a new signal.
Forge Network. Threat assessment update. New signatures detected. Three nodes in the southern network, two in the mid-line district. All flagged. All requiring an immune response.
She looks at the Gateway one last time.
She does not understand it. She has no framework for what she just perceived on the other side of it. She has exactly as much information about the Enigmatic Universe as she had before she walked into this hall, which is none. She has seen that a door exists. She does not know what is on the other side or how it connects to everything she has discovered underground tonight.
She turns away from it.
In the south yard of Styx Station: a rail car, still operational. The surface route south, toward the dark nodes, toward the places the network has just told her it needs something done.
She walks toward the platform.
The Goblin follows.
Behind her, the Gateway pulses once. A single throb of energy that travels through the hall, through the Ley lines, through the entire network from Styx to Corgemont and beyond. Not a message. A register. The way a heartbeat is not a message but a record of continued function.
The story has not ended.
It has barely started.
End of Episode VII — End of Arc One
Vex is down. The Swamp Wards are still flooding. The Goblin swarm is disbanding without a network directive to hold them together — they will scatter across the southern line, become variables in other situations. The Goblin from Corgemont stays. The rail car is running. There are five new threat signatures in the network. The Gateway is open behind her. Malvek has already received the report that Vex is gone. He is already recalculating. The network knew this would happen. It already deployed the next white cell. Somewhere in the southern line, someone else is standing next to an activation point, not knowing what it is yet.
